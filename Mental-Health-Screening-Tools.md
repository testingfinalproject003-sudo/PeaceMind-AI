# Mental Health Screening & Coping Resource

Reference document for validated mental health screening instruments and evidence-based coping exercises. Related to the PeaceMind AI project (see `Avoidant-Withdrawn-Implementation.md`).

---

## 1. PHQ-9 — Patient Health Questionnaire (Depression Screener)

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by any of the following problems?

1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
3. Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much
4. Feeling tired or having little energy
5. Poor appetite or overeating
6. Feeling bad about yourself — or that you are a failure, or have let yourself or your family down
7. Trouble concentrating on things, such as reading or watching TV
8. Moving or speaking noticeably slower than usual, or being fidgety/restless
9. Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way

| Response | Score |
|---|---|
| Not at all | 0 |
| Several days | 1 |
| More than half the days | 2 |
| Nearly every day | 3 |

**Scoring:** sum all 9 items. Total range 0–27.

- 0–4: Minimal
- 5–9: Mild
- 10–14: Moderate
- 15–19: Moderately severe
- 20–27: Severe

## 2. GAD-7 — Generalized Anxiety Disorder Screener

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following?

1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless that it is hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen

| Response | Score |
|---|---|
| Not at all | 0 |
| Several days | 1 |
| More than half the days | 2 |
| Nearly every day | 3 |

**Scoring:** sum all 7 items. Total range 0–21. Cutoffs: 5 = mild, 10 = moderate, 15 = severe.

## 3. PSS — Perceived Stress Scale (short form)

Questions ask how often, in the last month, the person felt a certain way (0 = Never to 4 = Very Often):

1. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were unable to control the important things in your life?
2. In the last month, how often have you felt confident about your ability to handle personal problems?
3. In the last month, how often have you felt that things were going your way?
4. In the last month, how often have you found that you could not cope with all the things you had to do?
5. In the last month, how often have you felt difficulties were piling up so high you could not overcome them?

## 4. Evidence-Based Coping Exercises

### 4.1 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

**How it works:** Sensory processing and anxious rumination compete for the same limited attentional bandwidth. Deliberately engaging the five senses interrupts the thought loop by redirecting attention away from racing or intrusive thoughts and into the present moment. Best used for panic, dissociation, or overthinking spirals.

- Name 5 things you can see
- Name 4 things you can feel/touch
- Name 3 things you can hear
- Name 2 things you can smell
- Name 1 thing you can taste

### 4.2 Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

**How it works:** A slow, controlled exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system (vagal tone), directly countering the sympathetic "fight or flight" activation that drives panic. This lowers heart rate and cortisol within minutes and is typically the fastest physiological brake on an escalating panic attack.

- Inhale for 4 seconds
- Hold for 4 seconds
- Exhale for 4 seconds
- Hold for 4 seconds — repeat 4–6 cycles

### 4.3 Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

**How it works:** Systematically tensing (≈5 seconds) then releasing each muscle group, head to toe, teaches the body to recognize the contrast between tension and relaxation — an awareness most anxious people have lost. Over repeated practice this lowers baseline muscular tension, which is itself a physical driver of anxiety.

### 4.4 Cognitive Reframing / Thought Records

**How it works:** Anxious and depressive states are maintained by automatic, distorted thoughts (catastrophizing, mind-reading, all-or-nothing thinking). Writing the triggering thought down, naming the distortion, and generating a more balanced alternative interrupts the automaticity of the thought and creates space to evaluate it rationally. This is the core mechanism of CBT (Beck's cognitive model).

### 4.5 STOP Skill (panic-specific, DBT)

**How it works:** Panic escalates through automatic reactive behavior. Forcing a deliberate pause before reacting is a pattern-interrupt that breaks the escalation cycle.

- **S** — Stop
- **T** — Take a step back
- **O** — Observe (thoughts, feelings, surroundings)
- **P** — Proceed mindfully
